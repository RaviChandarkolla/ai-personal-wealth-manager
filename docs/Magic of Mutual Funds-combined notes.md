## Session 1 - Introduction to Mutual Funds

### What is a Mutual Fund?

- **SEBI definition:** a mechanism for pooling the resources by issuing units to the investors and investing funds in securities in accordance with objectives as disclosed in the offer document.
- **Oxford Dictionary:** "An investment program funded by shareholders that trades in diversified holdings and is professionally managed."
- **Simple terms:** a number of investors who share a common investment objective pool in their money with the sole intention of earning returns.
- The pool is managed by a professional **Fund Manager**, who invests in various financial securities based on the fund's objective, aiming to produce capital gains and income for investors.
- Each fund has its own investment objective, set by the Fund Manager before launch. Based on it, the manager picks securities and sets return and risk expectations.
- Example: if the objective is wealth conservation, the manager invests mostly in Debt and Liquid instruments, which are less risky and give inflation-comparable returns.

Flow: Investors (pool their money) -> Fund Managers (select securities) -> Securities (generate returns) -> Returns (distributed among investors).

### History of Mutual Funds in India

| Phase | Period | Highlights |
|---|---|---|
| I | 1964-1987 | Unit Trust of India - single mutual fund entity in India |
| II | 1987-1993 | Seven new funds established, led by SBI along with CanBank, Indian Bank, BoI, PNB, LIC and GIC |
| III | 1993-2003 | SEBI formulates mutual fund regulation; Kothari Pioneer Mutual Fund becomes first private sector entrant |
| IV | 2003-2014 | UTI bifurcated into two separate entities; SEBI abolishes entry load for mutual funds |
| V | 2014-present | 45 mutual fund organisations; as on 30 April 2020, industry AUM stood at Rs. 23.93 trillion |

- In 1963, the Government of India and RBI formed Unit Trust of India (UTI). Primary objective: encourage and pool savings of middle and lower income groups and let them share the benefits and prosperity of industrial development.

### Structure of Mutual Funds

- Components: Mutual Fund (Trust), Sponsor, Trustee, Asset Management Company (AMC), Registrar & Transfer Agent (RTA), Custodian.
- In India, mutual funds are constituted as Trusts governed by the Indian Trust Act, 1882.
- It is a 2-tier structure: the mutual fund is created by one or more sponsors; the beneficiaries of the trust are the investors who invest in the schemes.

### Types of Mutual Funds (overview)

- Fund schemes: Open Ended, Close Ended, Interval Funds
- Management of funds: Actively managed, Passively managed
- Assets invested in: Equity, Debt, Hybrid
- Investment objectives: Growth, Dividend, Dividend Re-investment
- Mode of investment: Regular plan, Direct plan
- Other types: Index Funds, ETFs, Sectoral Funds, ELSS, International Funds, Goal-based Funds
- Detailed in Session 3.

### Why Invest in Mutual Funds

- Analogy: earlier generations made wealth through Real Estate and Gold. Gold still holds value, Real Estate has over-grown, and Equities are the new booming avenue.
- "Mutual Funds Sahi Hai!" (AMFI initiative): do not invest in mutual funds, invest *through* them.
- Investors have different requirements but lack time, ability and expertise to manage them. Mutual funds provide convenience: a team of experts manages the money.
- Industry growth from Rs. 10 trillion in 2014 to Rs. 23.93 trillion in 2020 - "It is the present to build the future!"
- Key question addressed: "My father/grandfather didn't invest in mutual funds, still they grew their wealth. Why should I?"

## Session 2 - Basic Terminologies & Concepts

### Assets Under Management (AUM)

- AUM is the total market value of assets or capital that a mutual fund holds on behalf of its investors. AUM of an individual scheme is also called **Fund Size**.
- Indicator of the size and success of the fund house and of investor confidence in a scheme.
- New investments or redemptions affect fund assets.
- A high AUM does not guarantee prosperous future returns, but does have an impact on investor profitability.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | SBI Mutual Fund | 373,968 |
| 2 | HDFC Mutual Fund | 370,113 |
| 3 | ICICI Prudential Mutual Fund | 358,142 |
| 4 | Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund | 247,757 |
| 5 | Nippon Life India Mutual Fund | 205,720 |

### Net Asset Value (NAV)

- NAV represents a fund's market value per share/unit; the price at which investors buy or sell units from an AMC.
- In India, NAV is calculated toward the end of every working day (values of bonds, deposits and stocks change daily), except for ETFs where NAV changes in real time.
- AMFI releases NAV data on its website after 8:00 pm.
- NAV is affected by changes in the prices of instruments held and their share in the portfolio. An equity-heavy fund's NAV follows stock markets; a debt-heavy fund's NAV follows debt markets.

**Is lower NAV better?** No - a higher or lower NAV is irrelevant.

- Investing the same amount in a lower-NAV fund gives more units, but the amount invested is the same.
- It is wrongly believed that a low NAV means "cheap" with a high probability of good returns. NAV represents intrinsic worth.
- For Dividend / Dividend Re-investment schemes, NAV matters: dividend is paid on face value, so a higher NAV gives fewer units and ultimately lower dividend.
- For Growth funds, scheme performance matters more than NAV; profitability depends on the fund manager's strategies. Follow the NAV trend over time.

### New Fund Offer (NFO)

- A first-time offer by a fund house for a newly introduced mutual fund; like an advertisement to attract investors and raise initial money quickly before the fund starts.
- Good option for Close-ended funds. For Open-ended funds, consider:
  1. Background check of the fund house
  2. No proven track record
  3. Asset allocation, expected returns, risks involved
  4. Read the offer document carefully
  5. Check for any lock-in period

### Benchmark

- A standard against which the performance of a security, mutual fund or investment manager is measured. A fund should at least beat its benchmark. It also gives an idea of the type of portfolio the scheme will have.
- Choosing an appropriate benchmark is important; the wrong index can lead to misjudging performance. Asset class, manager's style and scheme objective help identify a suitable benchmark.
- Since 2012, SEBI made it mandatory for fund houses to declare a benchmark index for each scheme.

Example - Axis Bluechip Fund vs Nifty 100 TRI:

| Metric | Axis Bluechip Fund | Nifty 100 TRI |
|---|---|---|
| 3 Year Trailing Return | 9.1% | 2.6% |
| 5 Year Trailing Return | 8.5% | 5.4% |
| Standard Deviation | 16.27% | 20.39% |
| Sharpe Ratio | 0.27 | -0.10 |
| PE Ratio | 43 | 25 |

The fund is less volatile and performs better than its benchmark, so it beats the benchmark.

**Price Return Index (PRI) vs Total Return Index (TRI)**

- Shares generate returns via capital appreciation and dividend payouts.
- PRI captures only capital appreciation and ignores dividends; funds, however, consider dividends in their returns.
- TRI includes both capital gains and dividends.
- SEBI circular (Jan 2018) directed fund houses to benchmark against TRI instead of PRI; all have complied.

### Load Charges

**Entry Load**
- A fee charged at the time of investing; fund specific. Investing in 5 funds meant paying it on each.
- SEBI abolished entry loads from 1 August 2009; there is no entry load on any scheme in India. A separate distributor fee such as transaction charge may apply.

**Transaction Charge** (allowed from 2011; one-time fee paid to the distributor; not deducted if the distributor opted out)

| Investor | Investment amount | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| First-time | Less than Rs. 10,000 | Nil |
| First-time | More than Rs. 10,000 | Rs. 150 |
| Existing | Less than Rs. 10,000 | Nil |
| Existing | More than Rs. 10,000 | Rs. 100 |

- SIPs with total outgo above Rs. 10,000: Rs. 100 charge, deducted in four equal instalments starting with the second instalment.

| Instalment | Fee |
|---|---|
| 1st | Nil |
| 2nd to 5th | Rs. 25 each |

**Exit Load**
- Charged if an investor exits before a specified time frame; discourages leaving schemes within a short period. Liquid funds do not charge exit load (they are meant for short term only).
- Each fund defines its own, mostly 0.25% up to 3% depending on type.
- Flat: e.g. 1.5% on redemption before 365 days.
- Staggered: < 365 days - 1.5%; 366-730 days - 1%; 731-1095 days - 0.5%; > 1096 days - Nil.

### Expense Ratio

- AMC expenses: fund management fee, agent commissions, registrar fees, auditor fees, advertising, etc. The fund house charges a fee called the Expense Ratio, irrespective of whether returns are positive or negative.
- SEBI maximum limits: Equity funds 2.5% p.a., Debt funds 2.25% p.a., Index funds 1.5% p.a.
- For equity funds, slabs apply (more AUM, lower ratio):

| Daily net assets slab | Ratio |
|---|---|
| First Rs. 500 crores | 2.25% p.a. |
| Next Rs. 250 crores | 2.00% p.a. |
| Next Rs. 1,250 crores | 1.75% p.a. |
| Next Rs. 3,000 crores | 1.60% p.a. |
| Next Rs. 5,000 crores | 1.50% p.a. |
| Next Rs. 40,000 crores | 0.05% reduction for every additional Rs. 5,000 crores |
| Balance of assets | 1.05% p.a. |

- It is not paid separately; it is deducted from daily NAV. Charged per annum but deducted daily.

**Impact on profitability** - Rs. 1,00,000 invested for 30 years at 15% CAGR:

| Expense ratio | Amount at end of 30 yrs (approx.) | Expense fees over 30 yrs |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | Rs. 66,00,000 | 0 |
| 2% | Rs. 39,00,000 | Rs. 27,00,000 |
| 1.5% | Rs. 45,00,000 | Rs. 21,00,000 |

A reduction of just 0.5% saves almost Rs. 6,00,000 in 30 years.

- **Regular plan:** invest via an intermediary (distributor, advisor, broker) who recommends and tracks your portfolio for a fee.
- **Direct plan:** investor manages on their own; no extra cost, so lower expense ratio.

### Market Cap

- Market capitalisation is the total market value of a company's outstanding shares. Share price alone does not indicate company size.
- Stocks are categorised as Large, Mid and Small caps. AMFI updates the categorisation six-monthly (end of June and December) in consultation with SEBI and exchanges.

| Parameter | Large Cap (1st-100th) | Mid Cap (101st-250th) | Small Cap (251st onwards) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Risk | Low | High | Very High |
| Probability of exceptionally high returns | Low | High | High |
| Liquidity | Very Good | Good | Low |
| Information availability | Very Good | Good | Poor |

### Portfolio Turnover Ratio (PTR)

- Indicates churn in the portfolio: how frequently assets are bought and sold.
- Calculated as the lower of new securities purchased or sold over a period, divided by the fund's total NAV.
- Lower PTR: long-term, buy-and-hold approach. Higher PTR: short-term approach, higher transaction costs that can raise the expense ratio.
- Higher turnover is generally seen in aggressive and growth funds; large cap funds generally have lower PTR.

### Tracking Error

- Annualised standard deviation of tracking difference data points; tracking difference measures how much an index product's return differs from its benchmark index. It tells the volatility of a fund against its index.
- Factors: Total Expense Ratio, Transaction & Re-balancing costs, Cash allocations, Timing.

## Session 3 - Types of Mutual Funds

Categories: Fund Structure (Open End, Close End, Interval), Management (Active, Passive), Assets (Equity, Debt, Hybrid), Objective (Growth, Dividend, Dividend Re-investment), Mode (Direct, Regular), Special Funds (Index, ETF, International, Solution Based).

### By Fund Structure: Open End vs Close End

| Aspect | Open End | Close End |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase of units | Anytime through the fund house | Not after NFO period; can be bought on secondary market |
| Maturity | Perpetual | Limited, based on fund objective |
| AUM | Changes due to investments, redemptions and underlying asset changes | Changes only due to change in value of underlying assets |
| Strategy | Active, passive or mix | Actively managed; as AUM is locked in, manager can take long-term calls |
| Systematic plans | SIP, STP, SWP available | Only lumpsum |
| Exit load | 0% to 3% on redemptions | None (no redemptions) |

- Open end: utmost liberty and flexibility to enter/exit. Close end: fixed timeline.
- Close end funds are listed on an exchange; exchange price can vary slightly from NAV.
- Close end suits investors comfortable with the horizon and selective bets for higher returns; open end is a good tool for growing wealth consistently.

### By Management: Active vs Passive

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Managers pick securities based on own research | Managers replicate an index (same stocks, same proportion) |
| Compared to benchmarks (mostly popular indices) | Replicate the index with minor tracking error (inflows/outflows) |
| Higher expense ratio (resources, expertise, research) | Lower expense ratio |
| ~94% of industry is invested in active funds | Only ~6% of industry AUM; gaining ground with consistent performance |

### By Assets: Equity, Debt, Hybrid

- **Equity funds:** invest in equity shares and equity related instruments; seek capital appreciation; highest risk.
- **Debt funds:** invest only in debt/fixed income (corporate bonds, T-Bills, CoDs etc.); low risk.
- **Hybrid funds:** invest in both equity and debt; suit medium/low risk appetite expecting higher returns.
- The portfolio is driven by the objective in the scheme document; always read the product leaflet/presentation.

### By Investment Objective

| | Growth Plan | Dividend Plan | Dividend Re-investment Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profits | Not paid directly; gain via capital appreciation | Stripped out of NAV and paid periodically | Stripped out of NAV and re-invested as units |
| Compounding | Benefit of compounding | Less compounding | Benefit of compounding |
| Tax | Only capital gains tax | Capital gains tax on redemption; dividends taxed separately | Similar to Dividend plan |

- Growth: long-term goals. Dividend: consistent payout. Dividend re-investment: hybrid of Growth and Dividend.

**Taxation of Dividend Plan**
- Old rule: Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) paid by the fund house - 28.84% on debt-oriented funds, 11.64% on equity-oriented funds; dividends were not taxed for investors.
- Budget 2020 abolished DDT. Dividends are now taxed in investors' hands at their income tax slab rate.
- Beneficial only for debt fund investors in lower tax brackets; higher bracket investors pay more tax. Consider taxability before choosing.

### By Mode: Direct vs Regular

| Direct Plan | Regular Plan |
|---|---|
| Invest directly with the fund house; no intermediaries | Invest through a distributor/intermediary; bear their cost indirectly |
| Lower expense ratio | Higher expense ratio |
| NAV usually higher | NAV usually lower |
| Via MF office, website, RTAs (CAMS, Karvy) or online portals (Groww, Zerodha Coin) | Via financial advisors, distributors or advisory firms |
| For knowledgeable, disciplined investors who research themselves | For others: professional advice, paperwork help, tracking, avoids impulsive decisions |

- There are more than 2000 primary schemes in India, so distributors help those without knowledge.

### Special Funds

**Index Funds** - replicate indices like BSE Sensex or Nifty 50; passively managed; judged by how closely they follow the index; suit investors seeking low-cost broad market returns.
- Pros: very low cost (lowest expense ratio among equity funds); least risky equity funds; diversification; easy to track (pick least tracking error).
- Cons: no manager flexibility; no returns above benchmark; limited options (mostly large cap indices); stuck with stocks until index composition changes.

**ETF (Exchange Traded Funds)** - basket of securities reflecting an Index, Sector, Commodity or Currency; trading value depends on underlying asset; bought and sold in real time at changing prices; no exit load.
- Types in India: Index ETF, Gold ETF, Sector ETF, Bond ETF, Currency ETF, Global Index ETF.
- Advantages: low cost; liquidity; transparency (daily portfolio disclosure); diversification; lower unsystematic (non-market) risk.

**International Funds** - invest in assets listed outside India; gaining attention due to low correlation with Indian markets.
- Country specific: e.g. Edelweiss Greater China Equity Offshore Fund, HSBC Brazil Fund, Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF.
- Region specific: e.g. Franklin Asian Equity Fund, Kotak Global Emerging Markets Fund, Edelweiss ASEAN Equity Offshore Fund.
- Commodity funds: global companies in mining, mineral exploration; e.g. DSP World Energy Fund, ABSL Global Commodities Fund.
- Real Estate funds: real estate companies or global REITs; e.g. ABSL Global Real Estate Fund.
- Global Gold funds: gold mining and extraction companies; e.g. Kotak World Gold Fund.

**Solution Based Funds** - facilitate investing for preservation of corpus or capital appreciation to fund specific future expenses (retirement, children's marriage/education).
- Retirement Planning Funds: invest in equity or debt per risk appetite; 5-year lock-in.
- Children's Gift Funds: capital appreciation for higher education, marriage or other needs of children.

### Value Funds and Contra Funds
- Per SEBI, a fund house may offer either a Value Fund or a Contra Fund, not both. Each must invest min. 65% in equity and equity related instruments.

**Value Fund**
- Manager invests in stocks undervalued based on fundamentals (trading below actual worth). Value stocks are frequently well-established dividend-paying companies.
- Distinct from Growth strategy (stocks with inherent potential to outperform the market).
- Ideology: market inefficiencies make some stocks trade below actual worth; managers identify them. Investment horizon could be higher than other categories.

**Contra Fund**
- Manager invests against the prevailing market trend in out-of-favour assets, selling them when they perform well. Far more complex than value investing.
- Targets neglected stocks that are undervalued today (low P/E or P/BV) but have long-term growth potential; portfolios hold defensive and "crushed down" stocks that gave negative returns in bear markets.

## Session 5 - Debt Funds

### What is a Debt Mutual Fund
- Invests in bonds (government or corporate), money market instruments, treasury bills etc. and passes returns to investors.
- A bond/debt is lending money to a company for its projects; in return you get regular coupon payments (generally annually), like dividends on stock. The primary goal of the bond market is long-term funding for public and private expenditure.
- Like FDs, you can lend to companies like REC, NHAI, HUDCO. Debt funds can also buy bonds not available to retail investors (e.g. Government of India bonds) and give a basket of bonds.
- Bonds are traded in the open market like stocks. Debt funds earn from price fluctuations plus coupon payments.

### Bond Price
- Bond prices change due to: change in interest rate, and change in credit quality.
- Interest rates and bond prices have an **inverse relationship**.
- Key takeaway: debt funds can make losses and give negative returns.

### Types of Debt Funds

- **Liquid:** Overnight, Liquid
- **Ultra Short Term:** Ultra Short Term, Low Duration, Money Market
- **Short Term:** Short Duration, Floater, Banking & PSU
- **Medium to Long Term:** Long Duration, Medium Duration, Dynamic Bond, Corporate Bond, Credit Risk, Gilt

**Liquid funds**

| Type | Horizon | Investment | Expected returns | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overnight | 1 day | Call money market | 3%-5% | Safest and most liquid |
| Liquid | 1-91 days | Mostly Treasury Bills | 5%-6% | High liquidity; exit load for first 6 days |

- Manager invests in high credit quality instruments and keeps average maturity minimal, reducing interest rate sensitivity. For substantial idle cash; can earn 1-2% p.a. more than a savings account.

**Liquid funds vs Savings account**

| | Liquid funds | Savings account |
|---|---|---|
| Risk | Very low | Almost none |
| Returns | 1 yr average 5%-7% | 3%-5% p.a. |
| Liquidity | Instant redemption; exit load for withdrawal before 7 days | Cash any time 24x7 |
| Taxation | Same as other debt funds | Per slab if interest exceeds Rs. 10,000 |

**Ultra short term**

| Type | Horizon | Investment | Expected returns | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ultra Short Term | 3-6 months | T-Bills, Commercial Papers | 5%-8% | Earlier Liquid Plus Funds; no exit load |
| Low Duration | 6-12 months | T-Bills, CPs, Deposits | 8%-9% | Only Franklin Low Duration and Baroda Treasury Fund have exit load; high return spread |
| Money Market | Up to 1 yr | T-Bills, CPs, CDs, REPO etc. | 6%-9% | Only SBI Savings Fund has exit load |

- Ultra short term funds invest in papers with maturity of 91 to 365 days.

**Short term**

| Type | Horizon | Investment | Expected returns | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short Duration | 1-3 yrs | Corporate bonds and debentures | 9%-12% | Low risk; exit load if withdrawn before 6 months |
| Floater | Usually up to 18 months | Min 65% in floating rate instruments | 9%-11% | Possibly high credit risk |
| Banking & PSU | Usually 12-18 months | Min 80% in debt of Banks, PSUs, Public Financial Institutions | 10%-12% | Medium-high interest rate risk; diversifies debt portfolio |

- Ideal for corpus not needed for 12-18 months. A floater fund puts part of the portfolio in floating-return and part in fixed-return instruments, reducing interest rate risk.

**Medium to long term**

| Type | Horizon | Investment | Expected returns | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long Duration | More than 7 yrs | Corporate bonds, debentures, G-Secs | 11%-12% | Very few funds; very high risk |
| Medium Duration | 3-4 yrs | Corporate bonds, debentures, G-Secs | 9%-11% | Medium risk; comparable to 3 yr FD |
| Dynamic Bond | Not fixed | All debt instruments | 11%-13% | High risk; can shift aggressively between maturities |
| Corporate Bond | Not defined | Min 80% in highest rated corporate bonds | 11%-13% | Medium risk |
| Credit Risk | Not defined | Min 65% in below-highest rated corporate bonds | 8%-10% | High credit risk; high spread in returns |
| Gilt | Not defined (usually 3 yrs) | Min 80% in Government securities | 11%-13% | No credit risk, but high interest rate risk |

- These funds lend to corporations for 3 to 8 years (a full or even two business cycles), making them extremely vulnerable to interest rate changes.

### Money Market Instruments
- The money market (as defined by RBI) is where short-term financial assets that are near substitutes for cash are traded; usually under a year. Characteristics: short maturity, high liquidity.
- Types: Treasury Bills, Commercial Papers, Certificates of Deposit, Repo/TREPs.

**Treasury Bills (T-Bills)**
- Short-term borrowing instruments issued by the Central Government; maturity under 1 year; assured yield and negligible default risk; issued via primary auction by RBI.
- Issued at a discount and redeemed at par; the difference is the investor's interest.
- Three types: 91-day, 182-day, 364-day. RBI publishes a quarterly auction calendar; auctions on the Negotiated Dealing System (NDS) managed by CCIL.

**Commercial Papers (CPs)**
- Unsecured, negotiable instruments issued by highly rated corporates to raise short-term funds for working capital directly from the market instead of banks. Usually not backed by collateral.
- Prior RBI approval is required. Gives issuers flexibility and liquidity, and investors an additional option.

**Certificates of Deposit (CDs)**
- Negotiable term deposits accepted by commercial banks from bulk depositors at market-related rates; usually in demat form.
- Issued to individuals, corporates, trusts, funds, associations. By scheduled commercial banks (except RRBs and Co-operative banks) at a discount to face value for 7 days to 1 year; for financial institutions: minimum 1 year, maximum 3 years.
- Minimum Rs. 5 lakhs to a single investor; above that in multiples of Rs. 1 lakh. No limit on total funds raised.

**Repo / TREPs**
- Repo: borrowing funds by selling securities with an agreement to repurchase on a mutually agreed future date at an agreed price.
- TREPs (Tri-Party Repo): a third entity (Tri-Party Agent) acts as intermediary between borrower and lender, facilitating collateral selection, payment and settlement, custody and management.

## Session 6 - Hybrid Mutual Funds

- Invest in both bonds and equity; diversified portfolio; suitable for moderate risk takers; popular for good returns and automatic rebalancing.
- Aim to achieve capital gains in the long run and generate income in the short run.
- Less risky than equity funds and give more returns than debt funds.
- **Advantages:** risk and returns balance, diversification, suitable for first-time investors, lower volatility, simplicity.
- **Disadvantages:** not risk free, difficulty in comparison, lack of focus.

Types: Conservative Hybrid, Balanced Hybrid, Aggressive Hybrid, Dynamic Asset Allocation, Multi Asset Allocation, Arbitrage Funds, Equity Savings.

### Conservative Hybrid
- 75%-90% in debt instruments; 10%-25% in equity and equity related instruments.
- Ideal starting point for a new investor; maximum capital protection with returns slightly higher than debt funds. Indicative horizon: 3 years or more. Do not expect returns as high as pure equity.

### Balanced Hybrid vs Aggressive Hybrid
- A fund house can offer either balanced hybrid or aggressive hybrid, not both.
- **Balanced Hybrid:** 40%-60% in equity, rest in debt; arbitrage positions not permitted; medium risk appetite.
- **Aggressive Hybrid:** 65%-80% in equity, rest in debt; focused on higher returns; high risk appetite.
- Investors with lower risk appetite get attracted to slightly higher returns of balanced funds and have a poor experience when markets correct.
- Very few balanced funds exist; the most famous are offered as FoF (e.g. ICICI Pru Asset Allocator Fund). Aggressive funds appear more attractive, so most fund houses prefer them.

- Equity and debt managed dynamically; allocations at the fund house's discretion.
- Invests across equity and debt to minimise risk based on market trends; e.g. if the economy is not doing well, reduce equity and move to fixed income or cash.
- Often compared with bank FDs; preferred by risk-averse investors willing to take some risk for marginally higher returns over 5-7 years.
- Allocations are not regulated, so performance depends on the manager's ability; screen the manager and history before investing.

### Multi Asset Allocation
- Invests in at least three asset classes with a minimum 10% each; the asset classes are not defined by SEBI. Indicative horizon: 5 years and above.
- For investors wanting a truly diversified portfolio; usually Equity, Debt plus another class such as Gold, Real Estate, ETF, Preference Shares, REITs. More than three allowed, but at least three must have min 10%. Foreign equity is not a separate asset class here.

- Follow an arbitrage strategy; per SEBI, invest min 65% in equity and equity related instruments. Less risky; suit short-term horizon of 2-5 years.

- Returns are not high; if there are too few opportunities the fund invests in stable debt securities.

## Session 7 - Guide to Select a Mutual Fund (Part A)

Steps: Check your risk profile -> Select the category -> Select the right fund house -> Historical performance -> Fund manager -> Other factors.

- Choosing is not a one-stop process; there are more than 2000 schemes. At each step there are winners and losers; the fund that stands the test of all parameters is the ideal option.

### Risk Profile
- Risk profile = **Risk Capacity** + **Risk Aversion**. Investors who are confident when investing may turn nervous when markets become volatile.
- Can be made on the AMC website or third-party sites like Moneycontrol (e.g. ICICI Pru MF Risk Profiling Tool).
- **Risk capacity:** ability to take financial risk; depends on investment horizon, liquidity needs, income and wealth, taxes etc.
- **Risk aversion:** psychological traits and emotional responses determining willingness to take risk and the emotional pain of losses.
- Investments are suitable if they fall within the borders of the risk profile.

### Selecting the Right Category

| Parameter | Large Cap | Mid Cap | Small Cap | Multicap | Debt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Risk | Low | High | Very High | High | Low |
| Probability of exceptional returns | Low | High | Very High | High | Low |
| Liquidity of underlying | Very Good | Good | Low | Mixed | Differs |
| Company information availability | Very Good | Good | Poor | Differs | Good |
| Indicative tenure | 5-7 years | 3-5 years | 7-10 years | 5-7 years | Less than 5 years |

- Equity-oriented funds suit goals more than 5 years away; debt funds suit short-term goals.

### Selecting the Right Fund House
- Largely a function of trust. SBI MF, HDFC MF, ICICI MF are known for institutional trust (backed by leading banks). Franklin Templeton India is known for credit risk strategies. PPFAS MF and Motilal Oswal AMC believe in long-term wealth creation.
- Understand each fund house's speciality and shortlist those aligned with your thinking.

### Compare Historical Performance
Types of returns: Trailing, Calendar, SIP, Rolling.
- **Trailing returns:** most popular; look backward from a date for annualised return over a period.
- **Calendar returns:** absolute returns for each calendar year; show consistency vs peers/benchmark.
- **SIP returns:** annualised yield for cash flows at various intervals.
- **Rolling returns:** series of returns over a period - several sets of 3, 5 or 10-year windows across a time frame. Captures market behaviour and impact of favourable/unfavourable events; not biased to a particular period; shows consistency, whereas trailing and calendar returns give point-to-point performance. Described as the most underrated parameter.

### Fund Manager
- Responsible for deciding and implementing the investment strategy and managing trading; the buy/sell calls significantly affect performance - "making or breaking" your investment.
- **Duties:** building the portfolio, complying with regulations, protection of wealth, monitoring performance of the fund, regularly analysing and monitoring changes in the portfolio, making timely disclosures.
- **Qualities of a good manager:**
  - Long industry experience; strong academic background
  - Consistently outperforms the benchmark
  - Check how long the manager has managed the shortlisted scheme; evaluate previously managed portfolios
  - Consistently higher Alpha (risk-adjusted returns)
  - Specialisation in a specific investment strategy
  - Stays ahead of and updated on markets

## Session 8 - Guide to Select a Mutual Fund (Part B)

### Other Parameters

**Age of the fund** - launch date appears in the factsheet. An older fund has seen major ups and downs, has more historical data and enjoys investor trust. Not the only criterion: some 5-7 year old funds have performed exceptionally (e.g. Axis Small Cap Fund, Parag Parikh Long Term Equity Fund).

**AUM** - indicator of popularity, only an initial indicator. High AUM may be due to consistent outperformance, early start, investment by large institutions like EPFO, or strong distribution. Higher AUM gives stability (no compromise on good opportunities due to shortage of funds), but may be too much to manage, forcing poor buys just to deploy the corpus.

**Sector allocations** - shows concentration in sectors; diversification reduces risk. Changes show which sectors the manager is bullish on; contra calls (investing in bearish sectors) can be attractive but risky. Shortlist only if comfortable with the strategy.

### Risk Ratios

| Ratio | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standard Deviation | Variation of past returns from their arithmetic mean; past volatility can indicate future volatility; lower is better. E.g. 12% average return and 3% SD: return could range between 9% and 15%. |
| Beta | Sensitivity of the fund to market movements (volatility vs benchmark). Beta 1: moves identically; beta 1.5: if benchmark moves 10%, fund moves 15%. |
| Alpha | Risk-adjusted performance; Alpha = Actual Return - Expected Return; excess return over benchmark. Alpha 1.5: outperformed by 1.5%; -1.5: underperformed by 1.5%. |

### Credit Risk / Default Risk
- Probability that the borrower does not repay interest or principal on the committed date. Measured by credit ratings from agencies like CRISIL, ICRA.
- Higher credit risk means lenders demand higher returns; if a fund claims unusually high returns, check credit risk first.
- Ratings change; a downgrade lowers the market price of the paper and affects the fund portfolio.

### Interest Rate Risk and YTM

| Measure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Modified Duration | Bond price sensitivity to interest rate changes (inverse relationship). Duration 2.5: for every 1% (100 bps) change in rate, price changes 2.5% in the opposite direction. |
| Average Maturity | Weighted average time for portfolio securities to mature; indicates sensitivity to interest rates and helps match your investment horizon. |
| YTM (Yield to Maturity) | Return if the bond is held to maturity. E.g. 8% YTM and 15 months average maturity: fund gives 8% if all bonds are held to maturity. Higher YTM can come from low-grade bonds. |

### Summary Checklist
1. Check risk profile and update regularly.
2. Select the right category and sub-category.
3. Shortlist a few trusted fund houses.
4. Compare historical performance.
5. Check in shortlisted funds: age, AUM, sector allocations; Standard Deviation, Beta, Alpha (equity funds); Credit Risk, Interest Rate Risk, YTM (debt funds).

## Session 10 - When to Redeem a Mutual Fund Investment

Hints it is time to redeem: completion of your goal; consistent underperformance; change in your risk profile; change in fund dynamics.

### Completion of Your Goal
- Goal-based investing keeps you focused and avoids mood-based blunders. When the investment nears the desired corpus, call it off to protect the corpus from volatility.
- Example: saving Rs. 10 lakh in a Large Cap Fund for a house down payment over 5 years; at the end of year 4 the corpus is Rs. 9.8 lakh.
  - Option 1: withdraw and move to safer avenues (Debt funds, FDs).
  - Option 2: stay invested till year 5, hoping to earn more.
- Option 2 may earn more, but one bad day can wash away gains - first make the goal safe. Then plan another goal (e.g. furniture).

### Consistent Underperformance
- Performance is relative; an apparently underperforming fund may not actually be poor. Notice, review, find reasons, put on watch list, and exit only when sure.
- Review: correct benchmarking, comparison with peers, period of underperformance, costs involved in switching.

### Change in Your Risk Profile
- Risk-taking capacity changes; an investment suitable earlier may not be ideal after 3-4 years. Tolerance can increase or decrease.
- Factors: age, psyche, earning members, dependents, life expectancy, regularity of income, employability.

### Change in Fund Dynamics
- If attributes you chose the fund for change and you are not comfortable, shifting is valid.
- Factors: change in fund management, change in investment style, merger of funds, regulatory changes, change in fund statistics.

## Session 11 - Things to Know Before Investment

Topics: who can invest, scheme related documents, KYC, how to invest, systematic transactions, taxation.

### Who Can Invest
- All individual and non-individual investors can invest unless a scheme is intended for a specific category. Always check the "Who can invest" section in the SID.
- **Individual:** adult residents, minors, HUF, NRI/PIO, foreign investors.
- **Non-individual:** companies registered in India; co-operative societies; trustees of religious/charitable/private trusts; partners of partnership firms; AOP/BOI; banks (including co-operative and regional rural banks), financial institutions and investment institutions; other mutual funds registered with SEBI; foreign portfolio investors registered with SEBI; scientific and industrial research organisations; universities and educational institutions etc.

### Scheme Related Documents
- **Scheme Information Document (SID):** fundamental characteristics (investment objective, asset allocation, fees, liquidity provisions); fund management team; risk factors; loads, plans, past performance, benchmark, riskometer; rights of unit holders.
- **Statement of Additional Information (SAI):** constitution of the mutual fund; information on sponsors, AMC, trustees; key personnel of AMC; registrars, custodians, bankers, auditors, legal counsel; condensed information on schemes launched in last 3 financial years; how to apply for NFO.
- **Key Information Memorandum (KIM):** summary of SID and SAI; key points for suitability decisions; most widely distributed; must accompany every application form; updated at least once a year.

### Know Your Client (KYC)
- All investors must be KYC compliant; establishes identity and address under Anti Money Laundering laws. Application must be accompanied by KYC acknowledgement.
- Steps: fill KYC form with supporting documents (PAN and address proof); In-Person Verification (IPV) is mandatory; form and documents are uploaded to a centralised KRA*; original documents returned after verification.
- KYC is a one-time exercise: once done through a SEBI-registered intermediary (broker, DP, mutual fund etc.) it need not be repeated.
- *KRA: KYC Registration Agencies registered with SEBI - CAMS, CDSL, KARVY etc.

### How to Invest
- Online/offline: directly with fund house, mutual fund distributors, registered investment advisors, R&T agents like CAMS/KARVY, banks.
- Online only: Zerodha Coin, Groww, Paytm Money, ET Money, KFinKart, FundsIndia.

### Systematic Transactions

**SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)**
- Invest a pre-determined amount at a regular interval (weekly, monthly, quarterly etc.). Ideal for disciplined investing by those with regular monthly income.
- Risk profile is equivalent to the asset type invested in, but SIP helps manage volatility.
- Units are bought at different NAVs: more units when the market is low, fewer when high - **Rupee-Cost Averaging**.
- Auto-debited from bank account; full and partial withdrawal possible during or after tenure.
- Types: Flexible SIP, Step-up/Top-up SIP, Perpetual SIP, Trigger SIP, Pause SIP.

**SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan)**
- Regularly withdraw a fixed amount; fund value and units reduce with each withdrawal. Amount and frequency (monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, annually) set by the investor.
- Withdrawals are normal withdrawals: check exit loads and tax implications on redemption. Start SWP after staying invested at least 1 year in equity funds and 3 years in debt funds to minimise tax.
- Cannot run a SIP and an SWP in the same fund. Number of withdrawals depends on withdrawal amount, corpus size and fund returns.
- Useful for those planning to retire soon - acts as monthly income.

**STP (Systematic Transfer Plan)**
- Transfer a fixed or variable amount or units from one scheme to another at regular intervals; amount and frequency set by the investor. Both schemes must belong to the same fund house.
- Types: Fixed STP, Capital Appreciation STP, Flexi STP.
- Uses: move from Debt to Equity (invest a lumpsum in debt and STP to equity during volatile markets); move from Equity to Debt when a long-term goal comes within 3 years, protecting the corpus.

### Taxation of Mutual Funds

- **Equity oriented:** schemes investing at least 65% of corpus in equity and equity related instruments.
- **Non-equity / debt oriented:** schemes holding less than 65% in equity.

**Capital gain** = sale/redemption value minus purchase value. E.g. Rs. 1 lakh invested on 1 April 2017 worth Rs. 1.5 lakh on 1 April 2020 gives Rs. 50,000 gain.

| Type of fund | Short term | Long term |
|---|---|---|
| Equity fund | Less than 12 months | 12 months and more |
| Balanced fund (equity oriented) | Less than 12 months | 12 months and more |
| Non-equity/Debt fund | Less than 36 months | 36 months and more |
| Balanced fund (debt oriented) | Less than 36 months | 36 months and more |

| Scheme | STCG | LTCG |
|---|---|---|
| Equity oriented | 15% | 10% (without indexation, on gain above Rs. 1 lakh) |
| Non-equity oriented | As per individual tax bracket | 20% (with indexation) |

- Capital losses on a mutual fund scheme can be set off against capital gains on another mutual fund investment of the same year; not against any other head of income.
- Short-term losses can be set off against both long- and short-term gains; long-term losses only against long-term gains.
- SIP: each investment is treated as a new investment. STP and SWP: each transfer/withdrawal is treated as a sale and taxed accordingly.

**Other taxes (FY 2020-21)**
- **Tax on dividends:** from 1 April 2020, dividend income from mutual funds (equity or debt) is subject to TDS at 10% if income exceeds Rs. 5,000; also taxable at the investor's slab rate.
- **Stamp duty:** from 1 July, 0.005% on the value of units on every investment (any scheme, ETFs, SIPs or STPs). Like an entry load; levied only on purchases, not on redemptions.

**Tax benefit of equity mutual funds (ELSS)**
- Deduction up to Rs. 1.5 lakh per annum under Section 80C; 3-year lock-in.
- Lumpsum: cannot redeem before 3 years. SIP: each instalment has a 3-year lock-in (e.g. instalment of 5 April 2020 ends 5 April 2023; 5 May 2020 ends 5 May 2023).
- With a 3-year SIP, on 5 April 2023 you can either start an SWP or wait another 3 years to withdraw a lumpsum.
